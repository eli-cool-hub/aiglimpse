Hospital AI Cuts Chart Completion Time by Nearly 40 Percent

Jefferson Health's ambient documentation system reclaims significant clinician time while maintaining patient satisfaction metrics.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
October 23, 2026

Philadelphia-based Jefferson Health has demonstrated substantial productivity gains from deploying ambient artificial intelligence for clinical documentation, with the heaviest users completing patient charts more than one-third faster than before the technology's introduction.

According to Becker's Hospital Review, a peer-reviewed analysis published in NEJM Catalyst Innovations in Care Delivery found that clinicians using Abridge's ambient documentation tool reduced chart closure time by up to 39.3 percent. The study examined 5,234 clinicians across Jefferson Health and Lehigh Valley Health Network, analyzing 283,028 ambulatory encounters conducted between July and December 2025.

Measurable Efficiency Gains Across the Board

The research tracked specific operational metrics among 637 clinicians who met active-use thresholds. The data revealed consistent improvements across multiple documentation categories:

  • Time spent writing notes per appointment declined between 10.5 and 17.3 percent
  • After-hours documentation time fell between 8.9 and 16.6 percent, a reduction clinicians call "pajama time"
  • Chart completion accelerated by 25.3 to 39.3 percent depending on the health network

The most prolific users experienced the greatest benefits. Top-quartile clinicians recovered nearly 17 minutes per scheduled day previously spent on documentation tasks and closed charts more than 36 hours faster than they had before adopting the tool.

Advanced practice providers gained more time than physicians from the system, recovering 9.9 minutes daily compared to 3.8 minutes for doctors. Despite these gains, weekly progress note volume remained stable or increased, suggesting the AI enhanced efficiency without compromising clinical documentation completeness.

Patient Experience Remains Stable

A critical concern for any clinical workflow technology involves potential impacts on patient care quality. The study found no evidence that ambient AI documentation harmed patient satisfaction scores. In fact, patient experience metrics were equal to or higher for visits where ambient recording occurred.

Researchers noted an important caveat: when comparing the same clinician's ambient-recorded visits against their non-ambient visits, the satisfaction advantage largely disappeared. This pattern suggests self-selection bias, where earlier adopters may have different baseline characteristics than later users, rather than demonstrating inherent superiority of the technology itself.

Real-World Impact on Clinician Burnout

The efficiency gains translate into substantial time recovery at organizational scale. Jefferson Health executives reported that the rollout has reclaimed approximately 1 million hours toward a broader institutional goal of reclaiming 10 million hours for patient care by September 2025. This initiative addresses the growing crisis of clinician burnout driven by administrative burden and excessive documentation requirements.

The study included oversight from Jennifer Stephens, DO, the lead researcher, and Baligh Yehia, MD, who serves as Jefferson Health President. Notably, Abridge, the AI vendor whose technology was being evaluated, had no role in the research and provided no funding, lending greater credibility to the findings.

The results represent one of the largest real-world evaluations of ambient clinical documentation technology within a major health system. As healthcare organizations continue exploring generative AI applications, this analysis provides quantifiable evidence that thoughtfully implemented ambient documentation can deliver meaningful productivity improvements while preserving clinical relationships and care quality.

Based on reporting from Becker's Hospital Review.
