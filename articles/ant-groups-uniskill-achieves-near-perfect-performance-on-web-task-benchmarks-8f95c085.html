Ant Group's UniSkill Achieves Near-Perfect Performance on Web Task Benchmarks

New approach trains AI agents to autonomously improve their own capabilities, reaching 98.4% success on complex interactive environments.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
October 8, 2026

Researchers from the Chinese University of Hong Kong and Ant Group's Qiantang Credit division have developed UniSkill, an AI agent framework that learns to both execute tasks and refine its own skill library without requiring expensive additional computational cycles. According to AI Weekly, the system achieved 98.4% success on ALFWorld, a benchmark simulating household environments, and 84.7% on WebShop, which tests navigation and purchasing on simulated e-commerce sites.

The advancement addresses a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning: how agents can improve their core capabilities through self-directed learning. Rather than relying on external feedback loops or retraining cycles, UniSkill employs a single unified policy that manages two objectives: executing assigned tasks and proposing modifications to its skill bank.

Solving the Evaluation Problem

The technical innovation centers on an elegant solution to a costly problem. Traditionally, evaluating whether a proposed skill modification improves performance requires running the agent through multiple environment simulations, consuming substantial computational resources. UniSkill sidesteps this expense by keeping both the current policy and previously recorded trajectories static, then measuring whether substituting the proposed skill for the original yields better outcomes across archived experience data.

This approach essentially transforms skill evaluation into a lightweight comparison problem. Rather than generating new environment rollouts, the framework reuses existing behavioral records to score proposed improvements. The efficiency gains enable more frequent skill refinement iterations within resource constraints.

Implications for AI Agent Development

The results suggest that autonomous skill refinement may be more tractable than previously assumed. Both benchmark performances rank among the highest reported for single-policy agents tackling these interactive environments. ALFWorld requires agents to understand natural language instructions and manipulate simulated household objects, while WebShop demands sequential decision-making through realistic e-commerce interfaces.

  • 98.4% ALFWorld success indicates strong capability in grounded language understanding and physical reasoning
  • 84.7% WebShop performance demonstrates complex multi-step planning and information retrieval skills
  • Unified policy approach eliminates architectural overhead from separate task and skill-learning modules

Broader Context

This work emerges amid growing interest in agent-based AI systems that can operate autonomously across digital and physical environments. Companies and research institutions have invested heavily in frameworks enabling agents to learn, adapt, and improve their own capabilities. UniSkill's approach of self-directed skill improvement without additional environment queries represents meaningful progress toward more sample-efficient and self-improving systems.

The research was published on Hugging Face, the collaborative platform increasingly used for sharing cutting-edge machine learning research. This accessibility may accelerate adoption and extension by other research teams exploring similar agent architectures.

UniSkill's performance levels and computational efficiency approach suggest practical applications in automation-heavy industries, customer service systems, and enterprise process automation where agents must handle diverse, context-dependent tasks with minimal human intervention.
