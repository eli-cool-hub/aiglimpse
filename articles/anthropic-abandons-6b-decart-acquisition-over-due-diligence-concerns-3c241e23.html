Anthropic Abandons $6B Decart Acquisition Over Due Diligence Concerns

The Claude maker's largest deal attempt collapses, signaling caution in AI infrastructure consolidation despite aggressive sector funding.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
September 8, 2026

Anthropic has terminated its proposed acquisition of Decart, an Israeli startup focused on chip optimization software, ending what would have been the AI research company's most significant corporate purchase to date. According to Bloomberg, the deal's collapse came after Anthropic completed its financial and operational review of the target company.

The transaction, valued at approximately $6 billion, represented Anthropic's fifth acquisition attempt this year. Had it closed, the deal would have expanded Anthropic's technical capabilities in a critical area: reducing computational overhead for model training and inference. Decart's software platform was specifically engineered to lower the infrastructure costs associated with deploying large language models, an increasingly important consideration as AI systems grow more computationally demanding.

The Startup Behind the Deal

Decart was founded in 2023 by Israeli technologists Dean Leitersdorf, Orian Leitersdorf, and Moshe Shalev. Despite its recent founding, the company achieved substantial investor confidence. In May 2026, the startup closed a $300 million funding round led by Radical Ventures, a venture capital firm known for backing infrastructure-focused AI companies.

The company's core offering addresses a fundamental pain point across the generative AI industry: the astronomical energy and financial costs required to train and operate state-of-the-art models. As compute requirements continue scaling, optimization software has become increasingly valuable to both frontier model developers and enterprises deploying AI applications.

Strategic Implications for AI Infrastructure

The failed acquisition raises questions about Anthropic's future strategy for vertical integration. The company has been pursuing multiple acquisition targets this year, suggesting an appetite for building out internal capabilities rather than relying solely on partnerships with chip manufacturers and cloud providers.

  • Anthropic's decision to walk away after due diligence suggests potential concerns about Decart's technology or market positioning
  • The collapse may reflect broader uncertainty about the value of efficiency-focused acquisitions in a rapidly evolving AI landscape
  • The $6 billion price tag may have been difficult to justify given integration risks and ongoing engineering uncertainty

The pullout comes amid intensifying competition in AI infrastructure, where companies like Crusoe Energy and others are similarly developing tools to reduce the computational footprint of machine learning workloads. Some investors and analysts question whether such efficiency gains can justify acquisition valuations of this magnitude.

Market Context

Anthropic's decision contrasts with its evident willingness to deploy capital into research and infrastructure development. The company has raised over $7 billion in funding rounds and has positioned itself as a serious contender against OpenAI and Google in the foundational model space. However, aggressive M&A activity often carries execution risk, and the decision to abandon talks suggests Anthropic leadership determined that integration challenges or technical concerns outweighed strategic benefits.

For Decart and its investors, the failed deal represents both disappointment and opportunity. The startup retains its $300 million in recent funding and may pursue partnerships or alternative exit strategies.
