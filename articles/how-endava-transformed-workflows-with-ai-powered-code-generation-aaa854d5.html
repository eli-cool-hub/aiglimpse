How Endava Transformed Workflows With AI-Powered Code Generation

Enterprise software firm cuts requirements analysis time from weeks to hours by deploying autonomous AI agents across its development operations.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
May 28, 2026

Endava, a mid-sized software engineering firm, has fundamentally restructured its approach to building applications by integrating autonomous AI agents into core business processes. The initiative represents a significant shift toward what the company calls an "agentic organization," where artificial intelligence handles routine tasks and accelerates decision-making cycles that previously consumed weeks of analyst time.

According to OpenAI, Endava deployed code generation models to automate substantial portions of requirements analysis, one of software development's most time-intensive phases. What once required weeks of back-and-forth communication between stakeholders now takes just hours, fundamentally compressing project timelines and reducing bottlenecks that historically delayed delivery.

Reimagining Software Delivery at Scale

The transformation extends beyond simple automation. By embedding AI agents throughout their organization, Endava created a new operational model where human teams focus on high-level strategy and creative problem-solving while machines handle the mechanical work of translating business requirements into functional code specifications.

This approach yields measurable outcomes:

  • Dramatically reduced time spent on requirements gathering and documentation
  • Faster iteration cycles for project planning and scope definition
  • Decreased cognitive load on senior engineers managing specification handoffs
  • More consistent code generation driven by standardized AI outputs

The Organizational Shift

Adopting agentic workflows required more than new tools; it demanded cultural and structural changes. Teams needed retraining to work alongside AI systems as collaborative partners rather than replacements. Project managers learned to orchestrate AI-driven processes. Developers focused on validation and architecture instead of boilerplate generation.

Endava's experience suggests that companies pursuing AI integration must think holistically about organizational design. Plugging new technology into existing workflows produces minimal gains. True acceleration happens when processes, incentives, and team structures align around AI capabilities.

Broader Implications for the Software Industry

Endava's success offers a blueprint for how established technology firms can compete in an era when AI augments human labor rather than simply automating tasks away. The company neither eliminated roles nor froze hiring. Instead, it reallocated skilled workers from repetitive analysis toward higher-value activities: code review, system architecture, client strategy, and innovation.

The case also highlights why code generation models have become central to enterprise AI investments. Unlike generalist language models, specialized code tools address specific pain points in software development workflows. Requirements analysis, while less glamorous than writing production code, represents a massive drag on project timelines and resource allocation.

For competitors and other enterprises watching this space, the lesson is clear: organizations that successfully integrate agentic AI systems will gain structural advantages in speed and cost efficiency. Those that treat AI as a peripheral add-on will struggle to capture meaningful benefits.

Endava's transformation from a traditional software services firm to an agentic organization demonstrates that the future of software development belongs to companies willing to rethink how humans and machines collaborate, not simply adopt the latest technology.
