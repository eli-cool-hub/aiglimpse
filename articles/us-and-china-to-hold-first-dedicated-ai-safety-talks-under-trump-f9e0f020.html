US and China to Hold First Dedicated AI Safety Talks Under Trump

Treasury Secretary Bessent will lead Washington's delegation to Beijing next month in a rare diplomatic engagement focused on artificial intelligence governance.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
September 5, 2026

The United States and China are preparing to convene their inaugural high-level dialogue exclusively addressing artificial intelligence safety, marking a significant diplomatic moment as Washington seeks to engage Beijing on critical technology governance issues. According to Reuters, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will represent the American government during talks scheduled for mid-September in Beijing.

The conversation reflects growing international recognition that AI development requires coordinated discussion between the world's two largest economies, even as broader geopolitical tensions persist. The talks represent a narrower focus than previous technology-related diplomatic engagements, concentrating specifically on safety frameworks and risk mitigation strategies for advanced AI systems.

Diplomatic Positioning and Protocol

Bessent's selection as lead negotiator underscores the Treasury Department's expanding role in technology policy under the current administration. His counterpart on the Chinese side remains under discussion, with two senior officials potentially representing Beijing's interests. Vice Premier He Lifeng, who holds equivalent diplomatic standing to Bessent, represents one possibility. Alternatively, Politburo Standing Committee member Ding Xuexiang could lead the delegation, particularly given his portfolio overseeing technology, artificial intelligence, and semiconductor policy within the Chinese government.

The choice of interlocutor carries symbolic weight in diplomatic protocol. Ding's participation would signal that Beijing views AI safety as a core strategic priority rather than a secondary economic matter, given his position coordinating China's technology infrastructure and innovation initiatives.

Context and Strategic Implications

  • This marks the first bilateral AI-focused safety discussion under Trump's return to executive office
  • Previous technology dialogues between the nations have addressed broader concerns including trade, intellectual property, and semiconductor access
  • The timing suggests both governments recognize AI governance cannot be deferred despite other tensions
  • Safety frameworks discussions could establish precedents for managing risks from increasingly capable AI systems

The diplomatic initiative arrives amid intensifying global debate over how to regulate and oversee artificial intelligence development. Concerns about AI safety span technical questions regarding model alignment and robustness, as well as policy matters including transparency requirements, testing protocols, and international coordination mechanisms.

Broader Context

Engagement between Washington and Beijing on AI governance remains unusual given their competition over technology dominance and AI capabilities. Both nations view artificial intelligence as strategically essential for economic competitiveness and national security. Previous attempts at technology cooperation have often foundered over concerns about intellectual property transfer, regulatory equivalence, and verification mechanisms.

The focus on safety rather than broader technology competition or market access suggests organizers hope to identify narrow areas of mutual interest. Both countries face similar challenges in managing risks from increasingly autonomous systems, though they may diverge significantly on implementation approaches and transparency requirements.

The September talks will likely explore whether common ground exists around fundamental safety principles, even if the nations maintain distinct regulatory philosophies and development strategies. Successful dialogue could establish communication channels for managing AI-related crises or misunderstandings as deployment accelerates globally.
