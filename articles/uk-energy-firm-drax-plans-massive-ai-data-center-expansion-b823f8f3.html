UK Energy Firm Drax Plans Massive AI Data Center Expansion

Three-way partnership targets over 1 gigawatt of capacity in Yorkshire, signaling major infrastructure shift for AI workloads in Europe.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
November 3, 2026

A major infrastructure project is reshaping the UK's position in the global race for artificial intelligence computing capacity. Drax Group, along with investment firms Brookfield and Radiant, announced plans to convert a North Yorkshire power facility into a sprawling AI data center complex capable of eventually exceeding one gigawatt of computational power, according to AI Weekly reporting on the initiative.

The partnership represents a significant bet on the continent's ability to host resource-intensive AI operations. According to AI Weekly, Brookfield estimates the full development could command more than 20 billion pounds in total investment if phased construction proceeds as planned. The announcement sent Drax shares surging more than 8 percent, reflecting investor confidence in the project's commercial viability.

Phased Rollout Strategy

Rather than rushing to completion, the three partners opted for a staged approach that manages both financial risk and infrastructure demands. The first phase targets delivery of up to 100 megawatts by early 2028, anchored by an initial 2.5 billion pound commitment from the consortium members. Subsequent phases would add roughly 500 additional megawatts, though timelines for these expansions remain contingent on market conditions and regulatory approval.

This measured strategy allows operators to validate demand from AI model developers and large language model providers before committing to the full scope. It also provides flexibility to adjust capacity specifications as computing requirements evolve across the sector.

Why Yorkshire Matters for AI Infrastructure

Choosing an existing power generation site offers distinct advantages for data center operators. Drax Power Station already possesses substantial electrical infrastructure and grid connections, eliminating the need to construct energy systems from scratch. This geographical location in Northern England positions the facility to serve European markets while maintaining operational distance from competing cloud providers clustered around London and other major tech hubs.

  • Existing power infrastructure reduces construction complexity
  • Grid connectivity supports multi-gigawatt operations
  • Northern location diversifies UK AI computing geography
  • Proximity to continental Europe appeals to multinational AI workloads

Broader Context for European AI Competition

This announcement underscores accelerating competition among nations and regions to secure data center capacity for AI applications. European governments have expressed concerns about dependence on US-based cloud providers for training and deploying advanced AI systems. A major facility in the UK could help domestic and European artificial intelligence companies scale operations without routing computational tasks across the Atlantic.

The project also reflects growing recognition that delivering capable large language models and other AI services requires enormous amounts of compute power, and therefore electricity. Data center operators increasingly view partnerships with energy providers as essential for meeting these demands economically and reliably.

Drax's pivot toward AI infrastructure marks a significant repositioning for the company, historically focused on energy generation and supply. The move positions the organization at the center of emerging trends reshaping data center economics across Europe and globally.
