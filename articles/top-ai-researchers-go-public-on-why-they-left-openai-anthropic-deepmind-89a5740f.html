Top AI Researchers Go Public on Why They Left OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind

Three prominent scientists detail safety concerns and strategic disagreements that prompted their departures from frontier AI labs.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
October 5, 2026

Three respected artificial intelligence researchers have broken their silence about why they departed from leading AI development organizations, offering rare public insight into the internal tensions shaping the industry's most prominent labs.

According to AI Weekly, Jacob Coxon, Daniel Kokotajlo, and Alex Turner recently participated in a joint interview discussing their decision to leave positions at major AI companies. Coxon, who conducted foundational model pretraining research at both OpenAI and Anthropic, formally resigned from Anthropic in September. Kokotajlo previously worked at OpenAI and now serves as executive director of the AI Futures Project. Turner, until recently, held a safety research position at Google DeepMind.

Core Concerns Driving Departures

The departures reflect growing unease among AI safety advocates regarding the direction and pace of large-scale model development. In his resignation announcement, Coxon raised concerns about what he characterized as a trajectory toward systems capable of rapid self-improvement, a capability that many safety researchers view with caution.

These resignations represent a significant moment for the AI sector. The individuals involved occupy respected positions within their respective organizations and bring substantial credibility to discussions about governance, risk management, and the strategic choices being made at industry-leading firms.

What Sets These Departures Apart

Unlike typical job transitions, these exits come with public statements outlining philosophical and operational disagreements. This level of candor is relatively uncommon among researchers leaving prominent positions in the competitive AI landscape, where non-disclosure agreements and professional norms often discourage detailed commentary.

  • Coxon's resignation emphasized concerns about development trajectories at both organizations
  • Kokotajlo's transition to independent research organization work signals a shift toward external oversight
  • Turner's move from DeepMind suggests concerns extend beyond any single company

Broader Industry Implications

The pattern of departures raises questions about whether leading AI laboratories are adequately addressing safety and governance questions that preoccupy their most thoughtful researchers. When experienced safety specialists and pretraining experts choose to leave, it often signals misalignment between individual researcher priorities and organizational strategy.

These moves occur against a backdrop of accelerating AI development, increased competition between labs, and growing debate about whether current safety practices sufficiently match the scale and capabilities of modern systems. The visibility of these departures may influence how other researchers evaluate career decisions and organizational cultures within the industry.

The researchers' willingness to discuss their concerns publicly may contribute to broader conversations about governance frameworks, safety investment levels, and the types of strategic choices that different organizations are making as the field advances. Their insights carry particular weight given their direct experience shaping current AI systems.
