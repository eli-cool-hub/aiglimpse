OpenAI Acquires Ona to Power Long-Running AI Agents

The acquisition bolsters OpenAI's infrastructure for enterprise deployments requiring persistent, secure cloud environments.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
June 11, 2026

OpenAI has announced plans to acquire Ona, a move designed to strengthen its technical capabilities for deploying autonomous AI agents across business operations. The deal reflects the company's push to extend Codex, its code generation platform, with infrastructure that can sustain extended computational tasks in controlled, enterprise-grade environments.

Infrastructure for Persistent Agents

According to OpenAI, the acquisition will equip the company with cloud infrastructure capable of maintaining AI agents that operate over extended periods. Current limitations in how AI systems are deployed have constrained their use in complex workflows requiring continuous operation, state management, and secure data handling. Ona's technology is intended to address these constraints by providing the underlying systems needed for stateful, long-running applications.

This move signals OpenAI's recognition that the next phase of AI adoption depends not just on better models, but on the operational infrastructure that allows those models to function reliably in real-world business contexts. Enterprise customers have increasingly demanded the ability to run AI agents over hours or days while maintaining security standards and preventing data leakage.

Codex Expansion Strategy

Codex, which translates natural language instructions into executable code, has become a cornerstone of OpenAI's developer-facing products. By integrating Ona's infrastructure, the company can expand Codex beyond one-off code generation into a platform for building and running agents that autonomously handle repetitive tasks, coordinate between systems, and learn from execution patterns.

The acquisition fits into a broader industry trend of AI companies building vertically integrated stacks. Rather than relying on third-party cloud providers for specialized functionality, OpenAI is consolidating capabilities that give it more control over performance, security, and user experience.

What This Means for Enterprise AI

The implications extend beyond OpenAI's internal roadmap. A fully realized persistent agent platform could unlock new applications across industries:

  • Automated customer support systems that maintain context across multiple interactions
  • Supply chain optimization agents that continuously monitor and adjust operations
  • Financial analysis tools that run autonomous research and reporting tasks
  • Software development workflows where AI agents collaborate with human engineers over extended projects

However, the technical challenges remain substantial. Maintaining reliability over long operational windows, preventing agent hallucinations from causing downstream problems, and ensuring security when AI systems interact with sensitive enterprise data all require careful engineering. Ona's existing solutions apparently address some of these concerns, though the specifics of its technology remain limited in public information.

Competitive Positioning

The move also positions OpenAI defensively against competitors developing similar capabilities. Anthropic, Google, and other AI labs are all exploring ways to deploy agents in enterprise settings. By acquiring infrastructure expertise, OpenAI aims to reduce time to market for agent-based products and maintain technological differentiation.

The timing matters too. As AI capabilities improve, the bottleneck shifts from model quality to deployment infrastructure. Companies that can reliably run agents in production environments will have significant advantages in capturing enterprise value from large language models.

OpenAI has not disclosed financial terms or a completion timeline for the acquisition.
