OpenAI and Poland's NASK Join Forces on AI Safety Research

The collaboration targets critical vulnerabilities in AI systems while building stronger defenses against emerging risks in the digital landscape.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
October 23, 2026

OpenAI has entered into a collaborative agreement with Poland's National Information Processing Institute (NASK) to jointly investigate artificial intelligence safety challenges and fortify cybersecurity measures across critical infrastructure. The partnership represents a significant expansion of OpenAI's international research efforts and underscores the growing recognition that AI governance requires cross-border coordination.

According to OpenAI, the initiative will combine institutional expertise from both organizations to tackle a range of technical problems spanning AI robustness, threat detection, and the development of more resilient systems capable of withstanding adversarial attacks. NASK, which serves as Poland's primary national cybersecurity authority, brings decades of experience protecting government and private sector digital assets.

Addressing Systemic Vulnerabilities

The collaboration focuses on several key research areas that have become increasingly urgent as AI systems proliferate across sensitive sectors. The organizations will examine how language models and other neural networks respond to malicious inputs, stress-test deployment pipelines for unexpected failure modes, and develop standardized evaluation frameworks for measuring AI system reliability.

By combining OpenAI's machine learning infrastructure with NASK's operational security protocols, the partnership aims to identify vulnerabilities before they can be exploited at scale. This approach reflects a broader industry trend toward proactive rather than reactive safety measures.

Why This Matters for Global AI Development

  • Establishes precedent for government-industry collaboration in AI safety outside the United States and Europe's major tech hubs
  • Creates shared research frameworks that could inform AI governance policies across Central and Eastern Europe
  • Contributes real-world testing grounds for safety protocols in diverse regulatory environments
  • Strengthens cybersecurity resilience in critical national infrastructure systems increasingly reliant on AI

The timing of this announcement reflects intensifying scrutiny of AI systems from both policymakers and security researchers. Recent high-profile incidents involving model jailbreaks and prompt injection attacks have raised awareness among institutional leaders that current safety practices may not adequately address emerging threat vectors.

Implications for the Broader AI Ecosystem

This partnership signals that organizations developing frontier AI systems face mounting pressure to demonstrate tangible commitment to safety research beyond internal efforts. By engaging established cybersecurity authorities, OpenAI positions itself as willing to subject its systems to external scrutiny and expert evaluation.

The collaboration may also serve as a model for how technology companies and national security agencies can work constructively on AI risk mitigation without sacrificing commercial innovation or institutional independence. Poland's role as a NATO member state with significant digital infrastructure concerns adds geopolitical weight to the initiative.

Industry observers note that partnerships like this one have become essential as AI capabilities advance faster than regulatory frameworks can accommodate. By investing in research infrastructure alongside government partners, companies can influence the trajectory of AI governance while gaining access to domain expertise unavailable in-house.

The two organizations have committed to publishing findings from their collaborative research, contributing to the broader scientific understanding of AI safety challenges and best practices for building more dependable systems.
