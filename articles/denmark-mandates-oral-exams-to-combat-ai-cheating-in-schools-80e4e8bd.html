Denmark Mandates Oral Exams to Combat AI Cheating in Schools

Copenhagen becomes first European nation to systematically address generative AI abuse in secondary education through verification interviews.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
August 8, 2026

Denmark has become the first European country to implement a comprehensive policy specifically designed to prevent students from submitting AI-generated work as their own. Education Minister Magnus Heunicke announced that all written take-home examinations in upper-secondary schools must now include oral defense components, with the directive taking effect when students return for the autumn term.

The policy represents a concrete response to an educational challenge that has vexed administrators across Europe since the widespread adoption of large language models like ChatGPT. While other nations have debated solutions, Denmark is moving ahead with mandatory verification interviews that require students to demonstrate comprehension of their submitted assignments.

Expanding Beyond Standard Tests

The oral defense requirement extends to the SSO, Denmark's signature written project that roughly 9,000 upper-secondary students complete annually. According to AI Weekly, this inclusion signals the government's recognition that AI cheating risks extend across multiple assessment formats, not just traditional examinations.

By requiring students to verbally articulate their work and answer substantive questions about its content, educators can more effectively distinguish between authentic student efforts and content generated by AI systems. This approach sidesteps the technical limitations of AI detection tools, which have proven unreliable and subject to false positives.

A Model for Educational Policy

Denmark's move carries significance beyond its borders. The country has positioned itself as a testing ground for AI governance in education, offering a practical template that other European systems might adapt. The simplicity of the approach, which leverages existing interview and presentation structures rather than introducing entirely new assessment methods, could make it appealing to policymakers in other nations.

  • Oral defenses allow direct evaluation of student understanding
  • Existing interview frameworks can be repurposed for verification
  • The method works regardless of detection tool reliability
  • Implementation requires teacher training rather than new technology

The underlying logic is straightforward: students who have genuinely engaged with source material and developed original arguments can articulate their thinking. Those relying primarily on AI text generation typically cannot answer follow-up questions that probe their reasoning or defend specific methodological choices.

Broader Implications

This policy also reflects a shift in how educators view the AI cheating problem. Rather than attempting to detect or block generative AI tools, which proves technically difficult, some administrators are redesigning assessments to make cheating impractical. Oral components, project-based learning, and collaborative work create friction for students attempting to pass off AI-generated submissions.

The Danish approach differs from proposed technical solutions pursued by some educational technology companies, which often rely on flagging suspicious text patterns or watermarking AI-generated content. Those methods remain experimental and controversial, raising privacy concerns and sometimes generating false accusations.

As AI capabilities continue advancing, educational institutions globally will need to decide whether assessment methods should evolve to accommodate these tools or whether verification procedures should intensify. Denmark's decision suggests a third path: restructuring evaluation itself to maintain academic integrity without technological arms races between detection and evasion.

The coming academic year will provide the first substantial data on whether this policy effectively reduces AI-assisted cheating while remaining feasible for schools to administer at scale.
