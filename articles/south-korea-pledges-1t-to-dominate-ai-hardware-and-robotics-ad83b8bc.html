South Korea Pledges $1T to Dominate AI Hardware and Robotics

Seoul's massive investment targets memory chip production, data centers, and humanoid robots as the nation races to lead the AI infrastructure race.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
June 29, 2026

South Korea is mobilizing an unprecedented $1 trillion investment across its government and leading technology firms to establish dominance in three critical AI infrastructure domains: semiconductor manufacturing, data center construction, and commercial robotics deployment. The coordinated push aims to position the nation as a global leader in physical and computational AI systems by 2028.

The initiative arrives at a pivotal moment for South Korean tech giants. Companies including Samsung and SK Hynix have reported record-breaking financial performance, driven largely by surging demand from the artificial intelligence sector for high-bandwidth memory components. However, this windfall masks a broader challenge: global memory chip shortages and elevated prices have rippled through consumer electronics markets worldwide, creating both opportunity and urgency for Seoul to expand production capacity.

The Triple Pillar Strategy

According to Ars Technica, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung framed the investment as a matter of national competitiveness during a televised address on June 29. The president characterized semiconductors, physical AI systems, and data center infrastructure as essential components for maintaining technological leadership in an AI-driven economy.

The three-pronged approach targets distinct but interconnected needs:

  • Memory chip manufacturing: Expanding production of DRAM and NAND flash components that power both AI training systems and consumer devices, addressing persistent global supply constraints.
  • Data center infrastructure: Building computational capacity domestically to support the increasingly resource-intensive workloads generated by large language models and other advanced AI systems.
  • Humanoid robotics: Accelerating the commercialization of advanced robotic systems for deployment in manufacturing, logistics, and service industries.

Hyundai's Robotics Ambitions

Hyundai Motor Company is spearheading South Korea's robotics push through its ownership of Boston Dynamics, the engineering firm renowned for developing sophisticated humanoid robots. The automaker plans to begin mass manufacturing these robots for deployment within its own automotive production facilities and to license the technology to other industrial operators. This dual strategy allows Hyundai to optimize manufacturing processes while creating a new revenue stream from licensing agreements.

The shift from prototype development to factory-floor deployment represents a significant milestone in the commercialization of physical AI systems. Rather than remaining confined to research laboratories, these robots are expected to assume responsibility for repetitive, hazardous, or physically demanding tasks across multiple industrial sectors.

Global Competitive Context

South Korea's announcement reflects intensifying international competition to control AI infrastructure. The nation's investment strategy acknowledges that dominance in artificial intelligence depends not merely on software capabilities but on securing the underlying physical systems: the chips that process data, the centers that house computing resources, and the robots that interact with the physical world.

For consumers and technology markets, South Korea's expansion of memory chip production could eventually ease the supply pressures that have kept component prices elevated. Success in this domain would ripple across the entire electronics industry, from data centers to smartphones.
