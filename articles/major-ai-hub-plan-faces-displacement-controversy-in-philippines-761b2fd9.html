Major AI Hub Plan Faces Displacement Controversy in Philippines

Human rights group flags resettlement concerns as US-backed semiconductor project expands in Southeast Asia.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
November 16, 2026

A sprawling artificial intelligence and semiconductor development zone planned for the Philippines is drawing scrutiny from international advocacy groups over its impact on local communities. Human Rights Watch raised concerns in mid-November about the Pax Silica initiative, citing potential displacement of hundreds of residents in the designated project area.

According to AI Weekly, the facility would occupy approximately 1,618 hectares within New Clark City, a special economic zone in Capas, Tarlac province. The Philippines formally joined the US-backed semiconductor consortium in April 2026 as its thirteenth participating nation, positioning itself as a key manufacturing hub for chip production and AI infrastructure development across the Asia-Pacific region.

Scale and Geographic Impact

The proposed complex represents one of the largest dedicated AI and semiconductor manufacturing sites in Southeast Asia. Its establishment reflects broader geopolitical competition to secure supply chains for advanced computing infrastructure, particularly as demand for processors powering large language models and other AI systems accelerates globally.

Documentation from the Bases Conversion and Development Authority, the government body overseeing the project, identified 92 individuals classified as "affected persons" residing in Barangay O'Donnell within the designated footprint. Local communities include both indigenous populations with historical land claims and agricultural workers whose livelihoods depend on existing land use patterns.

Resettlement and Policy Questions

  • Community assessment conducted in July 2026 documented residents currently inhabiting project land
  • No comprehensive relocation framework has been publicly disclosed
  • Indigenous land rights remain unresolved in relevant Philippine legislation
  • Environmental impact studies for the facility remain incomplete

The timing of the initiative coincides with expanded US strategic competition with China over semiconductor manufacturing dominance. Advanced chips underpin everything from consumer AI assistants to enterprise data center operations, making production capacity a critical strategic asset. The Philippine government has promoted the project as essential economic development that would generate employment and technology transfer.

Broader Implications for Tech Infrastructure

The Pax Silica controversy illuminates growing tensions between rapid AI industry expansion and community protection standards. As tech companies and governments race to build manufacturing ecosystems for the processors and hardware that enable AI systems, questions about environmental review, land rights, and fair compensation for displaced populations remain inadequately addressed in many jurisdictions.

"The documentation confirms the existence of affected communities whose rights and welfare must be protected before any project proceeds," Human Rights Watch indicated in their November assessment.

The situation reflects patterns observed in previous infrastructure mega-projects across developing economies, where local stakeholder engagement often lags behind planning and financing timelines. For the AI industry specifically, it raises questions about the full social cost of building out the hardware infrastructure supporting artificial intelligence expansion.

The Philippine authorities have not released a formal response to the human rights concerns, though project proponents emphasize the economic benefits expected from semiconductor manufacturing and AI development activities. Negotiations between affected communities, local government, and project developers are expected to continue in coming months.
