US and China Plot AI Safety Framework Ahead of Trump-Xi Summit

Washington and Beijing prepare high-level talks on artificial intelligence risks, signaling potential cooperation despite deepening tech tensions.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
September 7, 2026

The United States and China are positioning artificial intelligence safety as a critical diplomatic priority, with plans for substantive talks between Presidents Trump and Xi Jinping scheduled for late September in Washington. According to AI Weekly, the two nations intend to use the summit as a platform to establish preliminary guardrails around rapidly advancing AI systems, marking a rare point of convergence between Washington and Beijing amid broader technological conflict.

The proposed agenda reflects growing concern among policymakers on both sides about the intersection of cutting-edge AI development and national security vulnerabilities. American officials plan to emphasize the threat posed by AI-enabled cyberattacks, a concern that bridges both nations' strategic interests given the asymmetric risks such capabilities could pose to critical infrastructure.

Pre-Summit Preparatory Dialogue

Before the presidential meeting, a working-level session is scheduled for mid-September to lay groundwork for more substantive discussions. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will lead the American delegation and has publicly advocated for establishing communication protocols and safety standards between what he describes as the world's two dominant artificial intelligence powers. The composition of China's delegation remains unclear, though senior officials including Vice Premier He Lifeng or Ding Xuexiang may represent Beijing's interests.

This preparatory round signals that both governments recognize the need for structured dialogue on AI governance before tensions escalate further. The focus on working-level coordination suggests officials believe incremental progress on safety measures could reduce misunderstanding and establish precedent for future cooperation.

Broader Context of Tech Competition

The summit represents a notable shift in diplomatic tone, occurring against a backdrop of escalating restrictions on semiconductor exports, artificial intelligence chip access, and competing claims about technological leadership. Both the Trump administration and Chinese government have positioned AI development as central to 21st-century economic and military advantage, intensifying pressure on technical communities in both nations.

  • US concerns center on AI-directed cyberattacks and autonomous systems
  • China seeks reassurance against restrictions on AI research access and talent
  • Both nations recognize mutual vulnerability to uncontrolled AI proliferation
  • Safety frameworks could reduce escalatory risks without compromising core interests

Realistic Expectations for Outcomes

While the inclusion of AI safety on the summit agenda signals genuine concern, observers remain cautious about the likelihood of binding agreements. Previous attempts at US-China technology cooperation have foundered on verification challenges, competing national security interests, and fundamental disagreements about governance models. AI safety discussions may produce framework documents or commitments to ongoing dialogue without concrete restrictions on either nation's development activities.

The substance of any agreement will likely hinge on whether both delegations can identify specific, mutually verifiable measures that address legitimate safety concerns without requiring either side to sacrifice strategic advantages or disclose proprietary AI capabilities. Establishing shared definitions of acceptable AI behavior in military and civilian contexts remains a complex challenge given divergent values around privacy, surveillance, and state authority.

The September talks will provide a critical test of whether pragmatic cooperation on artificial intelligence safety can survive broader strategic competition between the world's two largest economies.
