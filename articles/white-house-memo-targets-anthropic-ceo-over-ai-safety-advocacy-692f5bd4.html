White House Memo Targets Anthropic CEO Over AI Safety Advocacy

Political pressure on AI leaders signals deepening divide over existential risk concerns in Trump administration circles.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
September 24, 2026

A policy document circulating within Trump administration circles this week singles out Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei as a prominent voice in what the memo's author characterizes as alarmist messaging about artificial intelligence risks. According to Axios, the internal memo, authored by a Trump political adviser operating outside formal White House channels, links Amodei and the effective altruism movement to what it calls an "AI-doom pipeline."

The memo's language reflects a broader ideological critique. It accuses effective altruism of prioritizing global and hypothetical concerns above national interests and domestic welfare priorities. The document specifically lists concerns about non-citizens, environmental issues involving non-human species, and potential future populations as areas where it argues the movement misdirects resources and attention.

Targeting Safety-Focused Leadership

The memo names both Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei, who serves as Anthropic's President, as central figures in what the author views as an unhelpful narrative about AI development. Anthropic, founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, has built its brand partly on commitments to AI safety research and responsible deployment practices.

This political scrutiny marks an escalation in tensions between the Trump administration and AI companies that emphasize caution regarding advanced AI systems. The timing coincides with broader policy debates over how aggressively the U.S. should pursue AI development relative to international competitors like China.

Effective Altruism Under Fire

The memo's critique of effective altruism reflects a political perspective that views the philosophical framework as misaligned with American interests. Effective altruism, which emphasizes using evidence and reason to determine how to benefit others as much as possible, has significantly influenced AI safety discourse and research funding priorities in recent years.

Key criticisms in the document include:

  • Assertions that safety-focused AI advocacy deprioritizes domestic economic interests
  • Claims that existential risk arguments distract from near-term applications and commercial deployment
  • Objections to the movement's international scope and values framework

Industry and Policy Implications

The emergence of this memo highlights deepening fractures in how different political and ideological factions view AI development strategy. While some policymakers argue that rapid innovation serves national competitiveness, safety advocates contend that advanced AI systems warrant careful governance frameworks.

Anthropic has previously defended its safety-focused approach as compatible with innovation. The company continues developing Claude, its flagship AI assistant, while conducting research on AI alignment and interpretability.

The memo's circulation underscores that AI policy has become entangled with partisan political dynamics, potentially influencing regulatory approaches and government relationships with major AI developers. Whether such internal critiques will translate into formal policy changes remains unclear, but the document signals that safety-advocacy positions face political headwinds in the current administration.
