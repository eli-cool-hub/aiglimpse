NYC Health System Deploys AI Platform for Remote Patient Monitoring

One Brooklyn Health adopts intelligent virtual care technology to predict adverse events and expand nurse oversight across multiple hospital units.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
July 6, 2026

One Brooklyn Health, a major New York City hospital network, has made a significant investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure by partnering with Hellocare.ai to modernize its remote patient monitoring capabilities. The deployment marks a strategic effort to enhance clinical decision-making and reduce preventable patient harm across the system's three facilities.

The implementation focuses on two core functionalities: AI-powered virtual nursing integrated with telehealth services, and autonomous virtual patient observation. These tools leverage machine learning models trained to recognize early warning signs of dangerous falls and skin breakdown, enabling clinical staff to intervene before complications develop. According to Becker's Hospital Review, the system continuously processes real-time patient activity data to identify deterioration patterns that might escape human notice during routine monitoring.

Scaling Oversight Across Hospital Units

One of the most immediate operational benefits is the ability for nursing teams to supervise patients across multiple hospital units simultaneously using a single AI-enhanced platform. This expanded oversight capacity addresses a persistent challenge in hospital operations: the resource constraints that limit how many patients any single nurse can safely monitor. By automating routine surveillance and flagging high-risk situations, the technology allows human clinicians to focus their attention where judgment and intervention matter most.

The AI platform's predictive capabilities target two of the most costly and preventable adverse events in hospitals. Fall-related injuries consume substantial resources and extend patient stays, while pressure injuries (bedsores) develop when immobile patients lack adequate repositioning and skin care. By predicting which patients face elevated risk, the system enables proactive prevention strategies rather than reactive treatment.

Broader Industry Adoption

Hellocare.ai, based in Clearwater, Florida, has already achieved substantial market penetration across American healthcare, currently supporting more than 100 health systems. This widespread adoption suggests that hospitals view AI-assisted monitoring as a maturing category rather than experimental technology. One Brooklyn Health's decision to invest in the platform reflects growing confidence that these systems deliver measurable operational and clinical benefits.

The three hospitals within One Brooklyn Health (Brookdale Hospital Medical Center, Interfaith Medical Center, and Kingsbrook Jewish Medical Center) collectively serve a population with complex healthcare needs and significant economic constraints, making efficiency gains particularly valuable. The implementation positions the health system to handle patient volumes more effectively while reducing the costly complications that drive readmissions and extended stays.

Operational and Clinical Implications

The deployment represents a broader trend of hospitals embedding machine learning directly into clinical workflows rather than treating AI as a separate analytical function. By integrating intelligent monitoring into the standard tools that nurses and care teams already use, One Brooklyn Health avoids the friction that often prevents adoption of new technologies.

The continuous real-time analysis of patient activity data creates a permanent layer of digital observation that complements (rather than replaces) human clinical judgment. This hybrid approach appears to address skepticism about fully automated systems by positioning AI as an enhancement to human expertise rather than a substitute for it.

Based on reporting from Becker's Hospital Review.
