Apple Adds Voice Customization to Siri in iOS 27 Beta

New controls let users adjust speaking speed and emotional tone as Apple overhauls its assistant with generative AI technology.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
July 6, 2026

Apple is rolling out fresh personalization options for Siri in the latest iOS 27 beta, allowing users to fine-tune how the voice assistant communicates. The update introduces granular controls over speaking pace and vocal expressivity, marking another step in the company's broader effort to reshape Siri around generative AI foundations.

According to TechCrunch AI, the new interface gives users straightforward sliders to adjust the speed at which Siri delivers responses, alongside toggles that control the emotional tone and inflection in its voice output. These adjustments represent a shift toward making the assistant feel more conversational and individualized rather than robotic.

Siri's AI Transformation

The customization features fit into Apple's wider initiative to rebuild Siri from the ground up using generative AI technology. For years, critics have pointed out that Siri lagged behind competitors like Google Assistant and Alexa in understanding context and handling complex requests. The company has been investing heavily in large language models and neural network improvements to address these gaps.

By allowing users to control vocal characteristics, Apple is acknowledging that different people prefer different communication styles. Some users may want rapid-fire answers while others need slower, more deliberate speech. The expressivity controls give the assistant room to sound more natural and less monotone, potentially making interactions feel less transactional.

What Users Can Expect

  • Adjustable speaking speed ranging from slower to faster delivery
  • Expressivity controls that add emotional nuance to responses
  • Settings that persist across devices in a user's ecosystem
  • Profile-specific preferences for different family members

The implementation suggests Apple is thinking about Siri as a more adaptive tool rather than a one-size-fits-all voice interface. Accessibility has long been a consideration for Apple, and these options could benefit users with hearing differences or those who process information at different speeds.

Broader Strategic Implications

These granular personalization options indicate Apple's confidence in its generative AI capabilities. The company has been cautious about rolling out AI features, focusing on on-device processing and privacy preservation. However, the iOS 27 beta release shows an acceleration in feature development and a willingness to experiment with user-facing AI controls.

The move also reflects competitive pressure. Other AI assistants have already incorporated similar customization options, and users increasingly expect to tailor AI interactions to their preferences. By adding these controls now, Apple is signaling that Siri is becoming a more sophisticated platform worthy of fine-tuning.

The iOS 27 beta is currently available to developers and public testers. These voice customization features will likely reach the general public when iOS 27 launches later this year, assuming testing reveals no major issues. Whether these adjustments alone address Siri's longer-standing limitations in natural language understanding remains to be seen, but they represent tangible progress in Apple's effort to build a next-generation voice assistant.
