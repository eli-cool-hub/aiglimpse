Tech Teams Show Strong Confidence in AI Agents for Enterprise Workflows

Survey reveals AI agents are gaining trust for complex IT tasks, but business context remains a critical bottleneck for broader adoption.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
June 29, 2026

Enterprise organizations are rapidly moving beyond AI pilot programs toward production deployments of autonomous agents, with technical teams expressing notably high confidence in the technology's ability to handle infrastructure and software tasks. According to MIT Technology Review AI, a new survey of 300 global technology experts ranks more than 100 specific workflows where AI agents are being trusted to operate with minimal human intervention.

The shift reflects a fundamental change in how companies view the business case for artificial intelligence. As pressure intensifies to deliver measurable return on investment, enterprises are turning to agentic AI systems that can manage entire workflows rather than isolated tasks. IT departments face particularly acute pressure: McKinsey projects that infrastructure costs will balloon by two to three times through 2030 even as budgets remain flat, creating an urgent need for automation solutions.

Where Confidence is Highest

Technical teams demonstrate the strongest trust in AI agents for well-defined, measurable tasks. Report data shows high confidence levels for processes such as automated report generation and boilerplate code creation. More significantly, confidence extends to complex multi-step workflows that require advanced reasoning capabilities.

Data operations emerged as a breakthrough domain for agent deployment. Teams express the most trust in systems where strong data structure provides a reliable foundation for decision-making. This includes monitoring data quality, detecting visualization anomalies, processing real-time streams, and profiling datasets. The pattern suggests that domain experts positioned closest to data generation can effectively guide agents by providing critical business context.

The Context Challenge

Despite overall optimism, a significant bottleneck limits agent deployment in more sophisticated scenarios. As task complexity increases, agents require increasingly robust business context to make sound decisions. This context-generation capability remains underdeveloped across the industry, particularly in enterprise environments where data is fragmented, difficult to integrate, and moves at speeds that outpace traditional development cycles.

Organizations struggle to supply agentic systems with the business intelligence they need to operate reliably in high-stakes scenarios. The gap between what agents can technically accomplish and what they can safely accomplish without better contextual information represents perhaps the most significant near-term constraint on wider adoption.

The Human Oversight Imperative

Successful agent deployment fundamentally depends on maintaining meaningful human oversight throughout the agent lifecycle. This finding carries particular weight given the financial and operational risks associated with automated decision-making in critical infrastructure environments.

Industry observers expect confidence in agent systems to accelerate as practical experience accumulates and enterprise technology environments mature. When agents operate within existing security boundaries, identity systems, and governance frameworks that organizations already use and understand, they become substantially more trustworthy.

  • Confidence surges for measurable, quantifiable tasks
  • Complex judgment tasks show growing confidence despite higher risk
  • Multi-step workflows and data operations present the strongest opportunity
  • Business context supply remains the primary technical barrier

The survey captures a pivotal moment in enterprise AI adoption. Technology teams are positioned to lead the transformation toward autonomous systems, but success depends on solving the stubborn problem of providing agents with sufficient business context to operate safely at scale.

Based on reporting from MIT Technology Review AI.
