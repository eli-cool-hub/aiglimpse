Flock Safety's AI Tool Contradicts Public Claims on Facial Recognition

Surveillance vendor's new search capability undermines years of assurances that its camera network cannot identify individuals.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
August 19, 2026

Flock Safety, a leading provider of artificial intelligence-powered traffic cameras used by law enforcement agencies across the United States, has quietly developed a search tool that directly contradicts its long-standing public statements about the limits of its technology.

According to AI Weekly, the company built a system called OS Investigate, previously known as Nightshift internally, that enables police officers to search its camera database using movement patterns and behavioral characteristics. The revelation emerged after journalists obtained and analyzed code from Flock's own systems, exposing the tool's underlying prompts, search interfaces, and operational workflows.

For years, Flock Safety maintained in public communications and marketing materials that its surveillance infrastructure "cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals." That messaging formed the cornerstone of the company's pitch to privacy-conscious municipalities and civil liberties advocates concerned about mass surveillance. The emergence of OS Investigate suggests this characterization no longer holds.

What the Tool Does

OS Investigate appears to function as a pattern-matching system that allows law enforcement to search across thousands of hours of video footage by inputting behavioral or movement descriptors rather than traditional facial recognition parameters. The tool represents a significant capability leap for police departments seeking to identify suspects or persons of interest across Flock's expanding network of networked cameras.

The system's existence raises uncomfortable questions about informed consent and transparency. Municipalities that deployed Flock cameras believed they were installing limited-purpose traffic monitoring infrastructure. The discovery of more sophisticated identification capabilities suggests a meaningful gap between what agencies understood they were purchasing and what the technology can actually deliver.

Implications for Law Enforcement and Privacy

  • Movement-pattern searching creates new vectors for both legitimate criminal investigation and potential abuse or misuse by authorities
  • The tool's capabilities may function similarly to facial recognition systems in practice, even if Flock avoids using that terminology
  • Departments using the platform may lack proper oversight mechanisms to prevent unauthorized searches or mission creep
  • The discrepancy between public statements and actual capabilities undermines trust in vendor transparency

Flock Safety has not yet publicly addressed the contradiction between its previous assurances and the documented functionality of OS Investigate. The company's silence suggests either a failure in communication strategy or a deliberate attempt to obscure the evolution of its product offerings.

This development arrives amid broader scrutiny of AI-powered surveillance tools and their deployment across American police departments. Regulators, civil rights organizations, and privacy advocates have increasingly questioned whether law enforcement agencies possess adequate safeguards against algorithmic bias or unauthorized use of identification systems.

The incident underscores a persistent challenge in the AI industry: the gap between technical capabilities and public representations. As machine learning systems become more powerful, vendors face pressure to market their tools as both effective and constrained. OS Investigate demonstrates how easily those competing imperatives can diverge, leaving governments and the public with incomplete information about the surveillance infrastructure operating in their communities.
