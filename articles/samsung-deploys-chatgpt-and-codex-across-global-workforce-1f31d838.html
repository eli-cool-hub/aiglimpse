Samsung Deploys ChatGPT and Codex Across Global Workforce

The electronics giant's enterprise-scale rollout represents one of OpenAI's largest corporate AI implementations to date.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
June 21, 2026

Samsung Electronics has become one of the earliest major technology manufacturers to integrate OpenAI's conversational and code-generation AI tools across its entire employee base. The deployment marks a significant milestone in enterprise adoption of large language models, signaling growing confidence among Fortune 500 companies in practical workplace applications of generative AI.

According to OpenAI, Samsung rolled out both ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex to workers worldwide, embedding these AI systems into daily operations. The move reflects a broader trend where established tech companies are moving beyond experimental pilots to company-wide implementation of language models designed to boost productivity and accelerate software development cycles.

Scope and Strategic Implications

Samsung's decision to pursue a global rollout rather than a limited trial suggests the company sees measurable value in generative AI for its operations. The dual deployment of ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex addresses distinct workplace needs: the former provides conversational assistance for research, writing, and analysis, while the latter specializes in code generation and programming tasks.

For a manufacturer of Samsung's scale, this deployment spans multiple divisions and geographies, creating one of the largest corporate implementations of OpenAI tools to date. The decision carries implications beyond Samsung itself, potentially encouraging other multinational enterprises to accelerate their own AI adoption strategies.

What These Tools Do

  • ChatGPT Enterprise offers enhanced security, longer context windows, and faster processing compared to standard versions
  • Codex assists software engineers by generating, explaining, and debugging code across multiple programming languages
  • Both systems can be customized to align with company-specific workflows and compliance requirements

Enterprise AI Momentum

Samsung's commitment underscores a fundamental shift in how large organizations view generative AI. What began as consumer-facing applications like ChatGPT has rapidly evolved into enterprise infrastructure, with companies increasingly viewing these tools as essential business systems rather than experimental technology.

The rollout also highlights the competitive dynamics within the AI industry. OpenAI's enterprise offerings now compete directly with alternatives from Microsoft, Google, and other vendors, each offering their own language models and integration strategies. Samsung's choice signals confidence in OpenAI's technical capabilities and business reliability.

Practical Applications

For Samsung's engineering and product development teams, Codex could accelerate software development by automating routine coding tasks, generating boilerplate code, and helping engineers understand unfamiliar codebases. ChatGPT Enterprise deployment might streamline internal research, documentation, and knowledge management across the company's diverse business units.

The enterprise focus also addresses legitimate concerns about data privacy and security that have deterred some companies from using consumer AI tools. ChatGPT Enterprise provides contractual guarantees around data handling and compliance, making it suitable for companies handling sensitive intellectual property or regulated information.

Looking Forward

Samsung's global deployment will likely influence other multinational corporations evaluating their own AI strategies. As more major companies integrate generative AI into core operations, the technology moves from novelty to normalized business infrastructure. The success or challenges of Samsung's rollout will provide valuable case studies for enterprises still deliberating whether and how to adopt similar systems at scale.
