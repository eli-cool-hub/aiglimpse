A New Wave of Startups Rejects Digital-First Culture

As AI funding surges, founders are building experiences designed to pull users away from screens and back into physical spaces.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
June 5, 2026

The artificial intelligence investment boom continues to set new records, yet a countermovement is emerging among venture-backed founders who are deliberately moving in the opposite direction. Rather than chasing the latest AI trends, these entrepreneurs are building products and platforms centered on face-to-face interaction and analog experiences.

According to TechCrunch AI, this shift reflects a broader reassessment of technology's role in daily life. Board, a new startup backed by Mirror founder Brynn Putnam, exemplifies this trend. The company is focused on creating social platforms and gaming experiences that require people to gather in person rather than engage through digital interfaces. This represents a fundamental departure from the prevailing assumption that every human interaction should be mediated through software.

The movement extends beyond traditional social platforms. The Cyberdeck community has gained significant attention for producing handcrafted, DIY computing devices that deliberately encourage users toward offline activity. These devices, which blend nostalgic computing aesthetics with modern functionality, have become emblematic of a growing desire to reclaim physical space from constant digital mediation.

Distinguishing a Movement from Mere Backlash

What separates this emerging trend from superficial anti-tech sentiment is its grounding in genuine product innovation. These founders are not simply rejecting technology. Rather, they are strategically deploying it to enable richer offline experiences. This marks a departure from earlier waves of AI-skeptical products that primarily focused on removing specific technologies without offering compelling alternatives.

The rise of these ventures occurs amid an unusual moment in startup culture. While artificial intelligence continues to command unprecedented capital flows and founder attention, there appears to be growing recognition that meaningful human experiences often resist technological enhancement. Some of the most engaged and passionate users are gravitating toward products that acknowledge this reality.

Implications for the Tech Investment Landscape

  • Physical experience design is emerging as a distinct product category worthy of serious venture investment
  • Founders are questioning assumptions about where technology adds value versus where it creates friction
  • User demand for offline-first experiences appears strong enough to sustain multiple well-funded ventures
  • This movement may presage broader recalibration in how investors evaluate technology's proper domain

The significance of this shift extends beyond individual companies. It suggests that after decades of relentless digitization, there is capital, talent, and genuine consumer appetite for alternatives. Whether this represents a sustainable countertrend or a niche market remains to be seen. What appears certain is that the conversation about technology's role in human flourishing is becoming more nuanced, with founders voting with their career choices and investors backing these bets with substantial capital.
