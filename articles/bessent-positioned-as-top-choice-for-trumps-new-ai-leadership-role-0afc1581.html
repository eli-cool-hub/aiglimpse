Bessent Positioned as Top Choice for Trump's New AI Leadership Role

Treasury Secretary emerges as frontrunner for newly created position overseeing artificial intelligence policy across federal government.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
September 22, 2026

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has solidified himself as the leading candidate to assume a newly created artificial intelligence leadership position within the Trump administration, according to reporting from multiple sources cited by Semafor. The role signals a major shift in how the White House plans to coordinate AI strategy across federal agencies.

Bessent's trajectory toward this position reflects his growing influence on technology policy matters within the current administration. Officials point to his active involvement in shaping AI-related decisions and his visibility on the international stage as key factors supporting his candidacy for the czar-level post.

Other Contenders in the Running

While Bessent leads the field, several other prominent figures are being considered for the position. The field includes:

  • Michael Kratsios, director of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, who maintains oversight of innovation and emerging technologies
  • Scott Kupor, head of the Office of Personnel Management, who previously worked at the venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)
  • Sean Cairncross, the National Cyber Director, bringing cybersecurity expertise to potential AI governance

Each candidate brings distinct experience to the potential role. Kratsios controls the machinery of federal science and technology coordination. Kupor brings Silicon Valley networks and private sector perspective. Cairncross offers national security and infrastructure protection credentials relevant to AI oversight.

What the Role Signals

The creation of an AI czar position reflects mounting pressure on federal leadership to develop coherent artificial intelligence policy. As AI systems grow more powerful and integrated into critical infrastructure, regulators face escalating calls to establish clear governance frameworks and coordinate responses across competing agencies.

Bessent's involvement in recent UN General Assembly discussions underscores the international dimensions of AI policy. As nations compete to establish technological leadership and negotiate AI governance standards, the Treasury Secretary's diplomatic engagement suggests the administration views AI strategy through both an economic and geopolitical lens.

According to Semafor, Bessent has already been serving as the administration's primary point person on artificial intelligence matters, indicating the role may formalize existing responsibilities rather than introduce entirely new duties.

Timing and Implications

The decision to create a dedicated AI leadership position comes as Congress continues debating potential regulatory frameworks for artificial intelligence development and deployment. Federal agencies including the FTC, SEC, and Department of Commerce have begun issuing guidance on AI governance, but coordination remains fragmented.

An appointed czar could theoretically streamline interagency coordination, establish clearer regulatory priorities, and position the United States in international negotiations over AI standards. The position also signals that AI policy has ascended to the highest levels of executive priority.

The selection process remains ongoing, with final determination likely reflecting broader strategic considerations around economic policy, technology sector relationships, and international standing.
