Google Acquires Wyoming Data Center Project From Crusoe Energy

Tech giant takes direct control of AI infrastructure site after startup fails to secure anchor customers for the $17 billion facility.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
November 7, 2026

Google has taken full ownership of a massive data center campus in southeastern Wyoming, absorbing a project that AI infrastructure startup Crusoe Energy had shepherded through early development stages. The Laramie County facility, which Google valued at $17 billion, represents a significant shift in how the search giant is building out its artificial intelligence computing capacity.

According to AI Weekly, Crusoe announced the transition on Thursday after months of behind-the-scenes negotiations. The startup had originally managed critical project functions including site selection, regulatory coordination with state and local authorities, and design of the power infrastructure needed to support the data center's substantial energy demands.

The transition reflects mounting pressures within AI infrastructure development. Google had expressed reservations about both the project timeline and escalating costs by June of this year, sources told Bloomberg. More significantly, Crusoe struggled to identify and lock in major customers willing to commit capacity purchases at the facility. This lack of confirmed anchor tenants created financial uncertainty for the venture.

Strategic Implications for AI Infrastructure

The acquisition underscores a broader industry trend in which hyperscale cloud providers are consolidating AI infrastructure development in-house rather than relying on specialized intermediaries. By assuming direct control, Google gains flexibility to design the facility specifically for its own model training and inference workloads, potentially optimizing for emerging large language model architectures.

Wyoming has emerged as an attractive location for data center expansion due to several converging factors:

  • Abundant hydroelectric power from regional water resources
  • Relatively low real estate and construction costs compared to coastal tech hubs
  • Supportive state-level regulatory environment for tech infrastructure
  • Existing grid infrastructure capable of supporting massive power draws

Crusoe Energy, founded by former Lonsdale Partners investment professionals, had positioned itself as a specialist in site development and power strategy for AI-focused data centers. The company identified what it considered optimal locations for compute-intensive operations and handled the complex regulatory approval processes required across multiple jurisdictions.

Market Consolidation in AI Infrastructure

This development illustrates how rapidly the AI infrastructure landscape is consolidating around established technology giants. Companies like Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta have increasingly brought data center development capabilities in-house, reducing dependence on external development partners.

The $17 billion valuation for the Wyoming site reflects the extraordinary capital intensity of modern AI infrastructure. A single facility of this scale requires not only enormous upfront construction investment but also long-term commitments to power supply agreements, transmission infrastructure upgrades, and ongoing operational expenses.

Crusoe's pivot away from the Wyoming project does not necessarily signal distress for the company. The startup continues pursuing other infrastructure opportunities and has raised substantial funding to develop next-generation cooling and power management technologies for data centers. The Wyoming transition simply reflects the practical reality that major cloud providers increasingly prefer to manage transformative infrastructure projects themselves.

Google has not yet disclosed specific details about the facility's timeline or capacity specifications, though industry analysts expect it will become operational within the next two to three years.
