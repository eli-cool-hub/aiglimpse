Healthcare Leaders Struggle to Define What AI Success Actually Looks Like

With only 4% of health systems proving AI value, hospital executives debate which metrics matter most for artificial intelligence investments.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
October 5, 2026

Healthcare organizations are pouring substantial resources into artificial intelligence implementations, yet most struggle to demonstrate tangible returns. The disconnect between spending and measurable outcomes has become acute: only a tiny fraction of health systems have successfully deployed AI at scale with documented results, leaving executives uncertain about which performance indicators deserve board-level attention.

According to Becker's Hospital Review, four out of five IT leaders report difficulty measuring AI's actual return on investment. This measurement gap reflects a broader challenge facing healthcare administrators tasked with justifying expensive technology rollouts to boards and stakeholders while simultaneously managing clinical operations and patient care quality.

Rethinking AI as an Operational Tool Rather Than a Standalone System

Rather than establish separate dashboards exclusively for AI metrics, several hospital system executives argue that artificial intelligence should integrate into existing performance frameworks. The most compelling approach focuses on tracking real-world outcomes that matter clinically and financially, rather than adoption rates or technical specifications alone.

Hackensack Meridian Health deployed an automated system for post-discharge patient outreach, which reduced hospital readmissions by 11 percent while simultaneously improving patient satisfaction scores. This tangible result demonstrates why outcome-focused metrics prove more valuable than mere deployment figures. The organization tied these performance benchmarks directly into leadership compensation structures, aligning incentives across the enterprise.

The key distinction emerging from healthcare leadership conversations involves separating adoption from impact. High usage rates for artificial intelligence tools do not necessarily correlate with improved clinical outcomes, patient experiences, or financial performance. An organization might deploy documentation automation across hundreds of providers while still failing to understand whether that deployment genuinely enhanced clinician satisfaction or reduced administrative burden.

Building Sustainable Measurement Frameworks

EvergreenHealth's leadership emphasizes connecting artificial intelligence investments to the core metrics healthcare systems already monitor religiously: quality and safety indicators, patient satisfaction surveys, financial performance benchmarks, and employee engagement levels. When organizations implement new technologies, they should track these fundamental measures both before and after deployment, isolating whether artificial intelligence specifically drove improvements.

This framework approach offers several advantages. It leverages existing data infrastructure and board familiarity with traditional healthcare metrics. It also prevents artificial intelligence from becoming an isolated cost center disconnected from strategic priorities. Finally, it forces implementers to articulate specific hypotheses about how a given AI tool will improve operations before deployment occurs.

Information Gathering as a Leadership Discipline

Beyond formal measurement systems, health system leaders increasingly recognize that staying informed about artificial intelligence developments requires deliberate effort. Following industry publications, engaging with respected technologists who hold diverse perspectives on AI's trajectory, and cultivating relationships with peers working on cutting-edge implementations provide context that formal metrics cannot capture.

  • Track established performance measures rather than technology-specific statistics
  • Monitor how artificial intelligence changes workflow efficiency and employee experience
  • Connect AI initiatives to patient outcomes and financial results
  • Build measurement discipline into leadership evaluation systems
  • Stay informed through diverse information sources and industry networks

The healthcare industry's maturation around artificial intelligence depends less on developing novel metrics and more on applying proven measurement disciplines to new technologies. Leaders who anchor AI investments to existing strategic frameworks will likely demonstrate clearer value and build organizational support for ongoing advancement.

Based on reporting from Becker's Hospital Review.
