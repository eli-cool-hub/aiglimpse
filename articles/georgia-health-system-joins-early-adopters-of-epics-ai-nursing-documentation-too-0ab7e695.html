Georgia Health System Joins Early Adopters of Epic's AI Nursing Documentation Tool

Northeast Georgia becomes fifth hospital network to test ambient AI that drafts clinical notes, aiming to reduce documentation burden on nurses.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
August 10, 2026

Northeast Georgia Health System has begun piloting Epic's Chart with Art, an ambient artificial intelligence tool designed to streamline nursing documentation across its five hospital campuses. The deployment marks the health system as only the fifth in the nation to implement the technology, signaling growing clinical adoption of AI-assisted charting in hospital settings.

The pilot program involves 15 nurses and two patient care technicians who are testing the system across the organization's facilities. Chart with Art operates by capturing real-time conversations between clinical staff and patients, then automatically generating draft documentation that includes assessments, care plans, and patient education materials. Nurses review all machine-generated content before it becomes part of the official medical record.

Addressing the Documentation Burden

Healthcare systems have long struggled with the administrative overhead that accompanies electronic health records. According to Becker's Hospital Review, reducing time spent on documentation remains a primary motivation for hospitals evaluating new technologies. "Our goal is to give nurses more time at the bedside," explained Marie DiFrancesco, chief nursing informatics officer at NGHS, highlighting the expected benefit of deploying the tool across clinical units.

Nursing documentation represents a significant portion of clinical workflow, with many studies suggesting nurses spend nearly a third of their shifts on administrative tasks rather than direct patient care. By automating the initial drafting phase, ambient AI systems promise to redirect attention and energy toward patient interaction and clinical decision-making.

Privacy and Clinical Oversight Protections

The health system has implemented multiple safeguards to protect patient privacy and maintain clinical quality. Key protections include:

  • Explicit patient notification when the AI tool is in use
  • Patient opt-out capability at any time during documentation
  • Required clinician review and approval before any AI-generated content enters the permanent medical record

These measures reflect broader industry concerns about AI transparency and the need for human oversight in clinical applications. Rather than replacing human judgment, Chart with Art functions as an assistant that accelerates routine documentation tasks while preserving clinician authority over medical records.

The Wider AI Documentation Landscape

Epic Systems, which dominates the electronic health record market, has positioned ambient AI as a core feature for its next generation of clinical tools. The technology leverages large language models to understand clinical context and generate medically relevant documentation without explicit data entry or voice commands. As hospitals pursue ways to address nursing shortages and burnout, solutions that reduce administrative friction have gained significant traction.

The Northeast Georgia deployment represents incremental but meaningful progress in translating AI technology from pilot projects into operational healthcare settings. With only five health systems currently running the tool, the technology remains in early adoption phases, though broader implementation could signal a shift in how major hospital networks address documentation workflows.

Based on reporting from Becker's Hospital Review.
