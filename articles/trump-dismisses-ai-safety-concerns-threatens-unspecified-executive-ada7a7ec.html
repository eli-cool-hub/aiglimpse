Trump Dismisses AI Safety Concerns, Threatens Unspecified Executive

In 60 Minutes appearance, president downplays industry warnings about AI risks while claiming DOJ enforcement.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
November 9, 2026

During a recent 60 Minutes interview, Donald Trump addressed longstanding concerns within the artificial intelligence sector about safety risks posed by advanced models, offering a notably dismissive assessment of the threats that industry leaders have publicly warned about.

When confronted with statements from AI executives emphasizing the potential dangers of their own systems, Trump responded by minimizing those concerns. "I think that the risk is not nearly as real as they talk, and I think the risk can be managed, and it's managed," he stated. The president suggested that federal oversight, specifically through the Department of Justice, was already adequately addressing any potential harms.

Enforcement Action Hint

Trump went further, suggesting his administration had taken action against an unnamed artificial intelligence company. According to The Verge AI, the president recounted: "We had a situation where somebody was reported to us to be doing something bad with AI. We called them very strongly and said, 'Get ready to go to jail. Please give your wife a kiss good night.'"

The identity of the targeted executive or company remained undisclosed. Trump only indicated that it was the leader of "one of the big ones" in the AI sector, refusing to provide additional specifics when pressed for details.

Broader Policy Positioning

The remarks reflect an emerging tension between Trump's regulatory stance and the caution expressed by major AI developers. Many leading companies, including Anthropic, OpenAI, and others, have publicly acknowledged risks associated with large language models and other advanced systems. Some executives have called for proactive government involvement in setting safety standards.

Trump's framing positions safety as a manageable issue rather than a structural challenge requiring new regulatory frameworks. This stance aligns with his broader deregulatory agenda, though it contrasts with recommendations from AI safety researchers and some industry insiders who argue that rapid capability advances warrant preemptive oversight measures.

What This Means

  • The administration's approach to AI governance may prioritize enforcement against specific misconduct rather than prospective regulation
  • Trump's willingness to threaten jail time suggests potential use of existing legal authorities against AI companies
  • The vague nature of the enforcement action raises questions about transparency in AI oversight
  • Industry players may face uncertainty about what behaviors trigger federal intervention

The comments also reflect a fundamental disagreement about how to characterize AI-related risks. While technologists and safety advocates debate whether current systems pose existential or near-term dangers, the president's position emphasizes the manageability of potential harms and downplays urgency.

Whether Trump's administration will pursue additional enforcement actions or develop formal policy guidance on AI safety remains unclear. The unidentified case he referenced, however, suggests that federal scrutiny of the sector is already underway, even if details remain restricted from public view.

Based on reporting from The Verge AI.
