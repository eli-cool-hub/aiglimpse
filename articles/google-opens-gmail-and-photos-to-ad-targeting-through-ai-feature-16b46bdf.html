Google Opens Gmail and Photos to Ad Targeting Through AI Feature

The search giant reverses a longstanding privacy commitment by allowing personalized ads based on personal content when users enable its new AI assistant.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
October 24, 2026

Google has fundamentally altered its approach to user privacy by creating a pathway for personalized advertising to access previously protected data. According to AI Weekly, the company modified its privacy policy on October 16, introducing an exception that permits ads to be targeted using content from Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos when users activate Personal Intelligence, an opt-in AI feature that powers Search and Gemini recommendations.

The policy shift represents a significant departure from a five-year-old commitment that had categorically prohibited personalized advertising from drawing on communications and personal files. The new language permits data harvesting for ad purposes specifically when users consent to Personal Intelligence, effectively creating a trade-off between AI capability and privacy protection.

What Changed

The revised privacy policy introduces a conditional clause to Google's previous blanket restriction. While the company maintains that personalized ads generally avoid these sensitive data sources, the exception carved out for Personal Intelligence users fundamentally reshapes that protection. Users who enable the feature implicitly consent to having their intimate digital content analyzed for advertising purposes.

The modification went largely unnoticed in mainstream tech coverage, with privacy advocates and independent researchers identifying the change after its implementation. This quiet revision suggests the company anticipated potential backlash from making such a policy alteration in a more visible manner.

The AI Trade-off

Personal Intelligence represents Google's answer to increasingly sophisticated competitor AI assistants. The feature enables more contextually relevant and personalized search results and Gemini responses by analyzing a user's complete digital footprint across Google services. The service requires access to previously siloed personal data to function effectively, creating the logical precondition for this policy change.

The company appears to be betting that users will voluntarily accept expanded data access in exchange for more powerful AI capabilities. This approach mirrors broader industry trends where privacy concessions have become prerequisite features for accessing advanced machine learning functionality.

Privacy Implications

  • Gmail content becomes analyzable for ad targeting purposes
  • Photo libraries and Drive documents enter the advertising targeting pipeline
  • Users must explicitly opt-in, though default settings and consent mechanisms remain unclear
  • The change affects billions of Gmail users globally

Industry Context

Google's pivot reflects intensifying competition in AI-assisted search and personal productivity tools. As companies race to deploy increasingly capable AI systems, data access requirements have become the central battleground. Privacy-preserving AI remains largely an aspirational goal rather than an industry standard practice.

The revision also signals how AI development may necessitate the erosion of historical privacy frameworks. Capabilities once considered impossible without extensive personal data now require regulatory and ethical recalibration across the technology sector.

This policy change underscores the tension between building powerful personalized AI systems and maintaining user privacy protections that consumers have come to expect.

Google's move will likely prompt regulatory scrutiny from privacy authorities worldwide and may influence how other technology companies articulate privacy commitments around their own AI initiatives going forward.
