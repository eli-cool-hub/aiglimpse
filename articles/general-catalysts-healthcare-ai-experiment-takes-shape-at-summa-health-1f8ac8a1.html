General Catalyst's Healthcare AI Experiment Takes Shape at Summa Health

Ohio health system names permanent CEO and assembles eight AI vendors to test a new model for hospital operations and care delivery.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
August 21, 2026

General Catalyst's transformation of Summa Health into an AI-driven healthcare organization is moving from planning into active execution. The Akron, Ohio-based health system has appointed its first permanent chief executive since the $485 million acquisition closed last October and unveiled a slate of eight specialized AI companies designed to overhaul everything from clinical diagnostics to administrative workflows.

Jennifer Eslinger, who previously led Rochester Regional Health in New York, assumes the role of president and CEO on September 21 following a national search. Her arrival represents a turning point for HATCo, the General Catalyst subsidiary managing the acquisition, which has spent months laying groundwork for what executives describe as a blueprint for modernizing American hospital operations.

The Eight Vendors Powering Summa's Transformation

According to Becker's Hospital Review, Summa announced on August 19 the eight General Catalyst-backed companies that will integrate their technologies into daily operations. The roster spans critical operational domains:

  • Aidoc and Verse Medical handle diagnostic imaging AI and post-acute care management
  • Clarium and Transcarent focus on supply chain analytics and durable medical equipment logistics
  • Commure and Fabric manage ambient documentation and care orchestration
  • Hippocratic AI provides virtual primary care services
  • Judi Health handles claims and benefits administration

HATCo is also integrating Percepta, another General Catalyst company, to create a unified "Health System Command Center" that will function as a central nervous system linking all eight tools into a single operating layer. The objective is to move beyond point solutions toward genuinely integrated intelligence.

Why Akron Matters

General Catalyst deliberately selected Summa as its proving ground rather than pursuing acquisitions in faster-growing markets. Daryl Tol, HATCo's president and CEO, explained at an Akron Roundtable event that the region's economic conditions and Summa's integrated insurance operations create a more rigorous test case. Success in a challenging market, executives reasoned, would carry more weight than proving the model in a booming health economy.

The strategy already attracted high-level attention. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. visited Summa in 2026 as part of his statewide tour, where discussions centered on AI-enabled care, behavioral health, chronic disease management, and addiction treatment coordination.

Clinical and Operational Priorities Emerging

Eslinger signaled a measured approach to AI implementation in conversations with industry observers. Rather than deploying AI across clinical frontlines immediately, she indicated a focus on easing administrative burdens first. The near-term strategy involves using AI to reduce back-office work before extending support to clinicians treating patients.

Tol highlighted a critical finding from his first year in Akron: Medicaid coverage losses are driving uninsured patients toward emergency departments for care that should occur in primary care or community settings. This operational insight is shaping how HATCo's vendors will be configured to address gaps in the care pathway.

The convergence of leadership stability, vendor partnerships, and clear operational priorities suggests HATCo's Summa experiment is transitioning from concept to measurable impact. Whether this AI-intensive model can improve outcomes while controlling costs in a challenging regional market will likely influence healthcare acquisition strategies industry-wide.

Based on reporting from Becker's Hospital Review.
