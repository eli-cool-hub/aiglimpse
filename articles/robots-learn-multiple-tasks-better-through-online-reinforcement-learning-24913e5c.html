Robots Learn Multiple Tasks Better Through Online Reinforcement Learning

New research shows foundation model robots can improve across diverse tasks simultaneously during deployment, challenging assumptions about narrow task adaptation.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
November 4, 2026

A team of researchers has demonstrated that robotic foundation models can simultaneously improve their performance across multiple distinct tasks during real-world deployment, moving beyond the single-task adaptation paradigm that has dominated the field.

The work challenges a fundamental assumption in robotics: that large pre-trained models, while capable of performing diverse tasks, can only be meaningfully improved on one specific problem at a time. According to arXiv preprint research, the team designed systematic evaluations and tested various algorithmic approaches to determine whether generalist robot policies could achieve what they call "multi-task, generalist improvement" in live deployment scenarios.

A New Framework for Robot Learning

Rather than assuming that robots deployed in real-world settings must narrow their focus to optimize performance on a single target task, the researchers explored whether the multi-task capabilities inherent in foundation models could be preserved and strengthened during the adaptation phase.

The investigation centered on three critical technical questions:

  • Can diverse tasks be improved simultaneously without performance degradation in any particular domain?
  • Which algorithmic choices, specifically around value estimation and policy extraction, enable this multi-task improvement?
  • Can robots generalize to completely new tasks not included in the online training process?

Key Findings and Practical Implications

The research produced unexpected results. When the team applied the right combination of techniques, robots not only improved on all tasks being trained simultaneously, but also demonstrated stronger zero-shot performance on entirely novel tasks that had never been part of the online improvement process. This generalization capability suggests that targeted online improvement can broaden a model's capabilities rather than narrow them.

The researchers identified that value-based reinforcement learning approaches were particularly effective, and that the distribution of training tasks during adaptation played a critical role in determining whether generalization would occur. These findings have immediate practical value for robotics teams attempting to deploy foundation models in complex environments where tasks frequently change or evolve.

The work represents among the first evidence that robot foundation models can undergo meaningful online generalist improvement, addressing a significant gap between the multi-task nature of pre-trained models and the typical single-task focus of deployment strategies.

Bridging Theory and Practice

The contribution extends beyond confirming that multi-task improvement is theoretically possible. The researchers provided detailed analysis of design choices that practitioners should consider, from resource allocation strategies to specific value estimation methods. This practical guidance could accelerate adoption of more sophisticated deployment strategies in robotics laboratories and companies.

The implications reach beyond robotics. As AI systems become more capable and operate in more complex real-world environments, the ability to continuously improve on multiple objectives simultaneously represents a significant step toward more adaptable and robust AI deployment. The research suggests that future robot systems need not make the binary choice between maintaining breadth of capabilities or achieving depth of expertise in narrow domains.

Based on reporting from arXiv (Michal Korniak, Marco Bagatella, Ralf Römer, Michal Nauman, …).
