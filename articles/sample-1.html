OpenAI ships GPT-5 Turbo, claims 40% gain on complex reasoning benchmarks

The new model debuts with a 2M-token context window, improved tool use, and a sharply lower price point, putting fresh competitive pressure on Anthropic and Google DeepMind.

By AI Glimpse Newsroom
·
May 25, 2026

OpenAI on Monday released GPT-5 Turbo, the latest iteration of its flagship reasoning model, claiming a 40% improvement on the company's internal evaluation suite for complex multi-step problems. The release lands a year after the original GPT-5 launch and represents the most aggressive push yet by the lab to defend its market position against fast-moving rivals.

The new model ships with a 2-million-token context window, double its predecessor, and what the company describes as "substantially improved" tool use, particularly for chained API calls and code execution. Pricing has been cut by roughly 35% on input tokens, a move that analysts read as a direct shot at Anthropic's Claude 4 and Google's Gemini 2.5 Pro, both of which have been gaining share in enterprise deployments.

What's actually new

Beyond the headline benchmark numbers, the technical changes break down into three areas. First, the training pipeline incorporates a new reinforcement learning phase that OpenAI says is responsible for most of the reasoning gain. Second, the inference stack has been re-architected to make long-context queries significantly cheaper to serve. Third, the tool-use system has been tightened to reduce the cascading-error problem that has plagued agent workflows.

"The story here isn't the benchmarks, those are always selective. The story is the price cut. OpenAI is signaling that they think they can hold the frontier and lower costs at the same time."

Independent evaluations from labs like METR and Scale AI broadly confirm the reasoning improvements, though with significant caveats. On long-horizon planning tasks, GPT-5 Turbo still trails Claude 4 Opus by a meaningful margin. On factual recall in obscure domains, Gemini retains the edge.

The competitive picture

For Anthropic, the timing is awkward. The company is reportedly closing a $5 billion funding round at a $60 billion valuation, and the pricing pressure from this release will compress margins on its enterprise business at exactly the wrong moment. Sources at two Fortune 500 customers told AI Glimpse they are already running competitive evaluations.

Google's position is more complex. Gemini's integration into Workspace gives it a distribution advantage no startup can match, but the Search team has been openly skeptical of deeper LLM integration in core products. That tension hasn't gone away.

What developers should know

  • The new model is available immediately via the API as gpt-5-turbo with the same tool-use interface as GPT-5.
  • Existing GPT-5 API calls will be automatically routed to Turbo by Q3, OpenAI said.
  • Pricing: $1.20 / 1M input tokens, $4.80 / 1M output tokens.
  • The 2M context window is supported but with degraded performance past roughly 800K tokens, per internal docs.

The release reframes the second half of 2026. The cost-per-intelligence curve just dropped another notch, and the labs that can't follow it down will start losing developer mindshare fast.
